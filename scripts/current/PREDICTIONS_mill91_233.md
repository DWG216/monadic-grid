# Pre-registration (2026-11-21): Millington pp. 91-103 and 226-233
N1 [id] Unified Cassini inverse: F(n-1) * F(-(n+1)) = 1 mod F(n)^2 for every n >= 3 (Millington's pairs 2*5 mod 9, 3*F(-6) = 3*17 and 8*F(-4) = 8*22 mod 25 are n = 4, 5).
N2 [id] Catalan products F(n-r)F(n+r) are +-1 mod F(n)^2 only for r = 1, 2 (because F(1) = F(2) = 1); for r >= 3 they are -(-1)^(n-r) F(r)^2, not a unit pairing. Explains p. 96 ("must always equal 1") and why the red line uses 2 * 13 (r = 2).
N3 [real] Mod 25 the multipliers 2, 3, 8, 13, 17, 22 are all primitive roots (order 20), so each Rodin-type diagram visits all 20 units and skips exactly the 5 multiples of 5 (his red dots).
N4 [real] p. 97-98 mirror rules: left-right pairs multiply to 1 (x <-> 1/x), top-bottom to -1 (x <-> -1/x); all 12 products he lists check mod 25.
N5 [real] p. 101-102: (a) L(n)/2 mod 9 equals F(n + s) for one fixed shift s with |s| = 6 (a quarter of the 24-cycle); (b) 2F(n) mod 9 equals L(n - s). Prediction: true for all n.
N6 [id] Epilogue 7: for U(n+1) = U(n) + U(n-1), U(n-1)U(n+1) - U(n)^2 = (-1)^n e with e = U(1)^2 - U(0)U(1) - U(0)^2 (= -29 sign convention for U0=1, U1=6 at n even per his tables: e = 29; U0=1, U1=4: e = 11). e is the norm of U(1) - U(0) phi in Z[phi].
N7 [real] Hence every inert prime (p = +-2 mod 5) divides e to an even power, for all 0 <= U0, U1 <= 60 (norms from Z[phi]); his 29 and 11 are split primes (= +-1 mod 5). Null: random integers of the same size violate this often.
