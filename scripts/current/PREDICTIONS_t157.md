# PREDICTIONS t157: t156 with the field acting back on its source

Registered 2026-11-26, before t157_feedback.py was written or run.

Found while reading Tilloy and Diósi (PRD 2016; PRD 96, 104045 (2017)). In their models the noisy Newtonian field acts on all matter, including the matter that sources it, so the matter's total decoherence has two parts: one from the record (measurement back-action) and one from the field's noise (feedback). t156 sampled Φ but did not let Φ act back on the source. Their principle of least decoherence picks the measurement strength that minimises the total, and the minimum is the Diósi–Penrose model.

Prediction [M]. With t156's kernels, D₀ = (πħG/4)K and the record's part E_G/(πħ), the feedback Hamiltonian ∫m̂Φ with white Φ adds (1/2ħ²)Δmᵀ D₀ Δm = (π/4)E_G/ħ. Total: (1/π + π/4) E_G/ħ = 1.1036 E_G/ħ, 10% above Diósi–Penrose. Over all measurement strengths the total is a + 1/(4a) in units of E_G/ħ, minimised at a = 1/2 with total exactly E_G/ħ (Diósi–Penrose).
S1 [M]: unravelling of t156 with the feedback Hamiltonian included (one mass, two sites, 20,000 trajectories): ensemble coherence decays at 1.1036 E_G/ħ within 2%.
P1 [M]: minimum of the total over measurement strength: E_G/ħ at a = 1/2 (scan of 41 strengths, analytic and simulated at three).
P2 [M, numbers]: Gran Sasso bound with the total kernel: R₀ > 0.54 × 1.1036^(1/3) = 0.558 Å (t156 kernels) or 0.54 Å (least decoherence).
P3 [M]: t156's Bohr-radius proposal (R₀ = 0.529 Å) is then excluded at the present bound in both cases (signal 1.17 or 1.06 of the bound).
Consequence fixed in advance: the claim "the grid's gravitational decoherence is 1/π of Diósi–Penrose" (t146, t149 P4, t156 P2–P3, the Oppenheim draft) is withdrawn: E_G/(πħ) is the record's part only, and the total is at least the Diósi–Penrose rate.
