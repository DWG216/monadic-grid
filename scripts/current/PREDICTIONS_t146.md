# t146 The reduction time (pre-registered 2026-11-25, before any t146 code)

Derivation (labels as in the theory doc). [S] The record writes one meeting term (one geometry) per beat (§1). [S] Two branches of a displaced mass are one record only while no record distinguishes them; they are distinguished when they become orthogonal under the generator that separates them. [chosen] That generator is the meeting energy, with gap between the branches equal to E_G, the gravitational self-energy of the difference of their mass distributions (the Diósi–Penrose quantity). [S] Quantum speed limit (Mandelstam–Tamm): an equal superposition across a gap E_G has ΔE = E_G/2 and reaches an orthogonal state no sooner than πħ/(2ΔE). Hence τ = πħ/E_G: the time at which the relative phase reaches π, the same π as the per-beat Nyquist edge of t133. Penrose's estimate is ħ/E_G with the factor left open; this fixes it at π.

P1 [M] In the two-branch model with gap E_G, the overlap |⟨ψ(0)|ψ(t)⟩| first reaches 0 at t = πħ/E_G (relative error < 1e-9), and over 10⁴ random states of 2 to 8 levels with energy spread ΔE none reaches orthogonality before πħ/(2ΔE) (the bound is never beaten, and is met by the two-level state).
P2 [M] Uniform sphere, mass m, radius R, displaced by d: E_G = (Gm²/R)(2λ² − 3λ³/2 + λ⁵/5), λ = d/2R (d ≤ 2R), and Gm²(6/(5R) − 1/d) beyond; the two forms agree at d = 2R to 1e-12, and a direct numerical integration of the difference field's self-energy matches the closed form within 1% at λ = 0.25, 0.5, 1.
Reported (no prediction): τ for benchmark masses (10⁻¹⁴ kg at 2000 kg/m³ displaced by its diameter; a 10⁻¹⁷ kg sphere; a 1 μg sphere at 0.1 nm), beside ħ/E_G.

Consequence fixed now: if P1 and P2 pass, the grid's reduction time is τ = πħ/E_G given the chosen gap; the factor π is a sharp difference from the usual ħ/E_G that experiments on mesoscopic superpositions can test. The chosen step (gap = E_G) remains to be derived from the record before it is stated as [S].
