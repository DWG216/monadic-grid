# PREDICTIONS t194: the up chord read where its hands are joined

Registered 2026-11-28, committed before t194_join_scale.py is written or run.

Derivation of the scale [S]. A mass is the rate at which a pair's two hands exchange (§108); the Higgs's value v = 246.22 GeV is that rate's scale for every sector. The record reads a joined pair as joined only at grains coarser than the join, and the count n is a property of the join (§335–§337). So each chord is to be read at μ = v. Down and lepton chords do not move with scale (t192–t193), so for them the choice is idle; for the up chord, whose top voice retunes itself (§338), it is the prediction.

Disclosure: t192 gave Q_u at M_t (0.8878) and 10⁵ GeV (0.8899), so Q_u(v) is roughly foreseeable; P1 is a consistency record. P2 states the same law as a mass. S1 reports how wide a band of scales the present errors allow, which is the honest measure of how much this tests.

Data and method: Huang–Zhou full-SM rows (t192). Masses at v by linear interpolation of ln m in ln μ between M_t and 10⁵ GeV, errors likewise; 10⁵ draws.
P1 [M]: Q_u(v) within 1σ of 8/9.
P2 [M]: with Q = 8/9 exactly, m_t(v) and m_u(v) fix the chord (A, δ); the predicted m_c(v) lies within 1σ of the interpolated m_c(v).
S1 [report]: the range of scales (from the six rows, interpolated) where Q_u is within 1σ of 8/9.
Consequence fixed now: P1 and P2 pass → the up count holds where the hands are joined; S1 states what a sharper m_c/m_t would decide. A fail → v is not the scale of the count, and the up chord's scale stays open.
Gate: statement about nature; public.

## Scoring (2026-11-28), run 1 (t194_run1.log)
- P1 PASS: Q_u(v) = 0.8879 ± 0.0014 (z = −0.66).
- P2 PASS: with Q = 8/9, m_t(v) and m_u(v) give m_c(v) = 0.5722 ± 0.0055 GeV against 0.5838 ± 0.0168 (z = −0.66).
- S1: Q_u is within 1σ of 8/9 for 10^1.96 to 10^5.96 GeV. The present errors allow four decades; v sits inside, but so does every scale from M_Z to 10⁶ GeV.
Verdict as fixed in advance: the up count holds where the hands are joined. The test is weak (S1). The prediction is three times sharper than the data: m_c(v) = 0.572(6) GeV.
