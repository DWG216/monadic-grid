# PREDICTIONS t187: the record's own ratio for each interval (back to the grid)

Registered 2026-11-28, committed before t187_grid_ratios.py is written or run. Disclosure: the coset minima for the tritone and the minor seventh were worked out mentally while designing (25/18 and 9/5); the rule was fixed before that, from the ground.

Rule from the ground [S]. The grid's arithmetic lives on the clocks of 2, 3 and 5 (its groups 2T, 2O, 2I; the Babylonian regular numbers): the record's ratios are products of 2, 3 and 5 only (7 is on no clock of the grid). An interval class k of the 12-note octave is the coset of the Tonnetz map {(b, c) : 7b + 4c ≡ k mod 12} in the lattice of 3s and 5s (t183, t184). Nothing privileges any member of the coset except how soon its record repeats, so the record's ratio for k is the member with the smallest D = √(pq) (octave-reduced). No tolerance, no choice of ratios from outside. The tempered tritone is exactly √2, the diagonal of the square (YBC 7289): no ratio of the grid equals it, so its record never repeats; it is also the balanced pair of pitch classes (t182) and the one interval its own inversion (the hand swap).

P1 [M]: the rule returns the traditional just ratios for every interval except the minor seventh and the tritone: 16/15, 9/8, 6/5, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 8/5, 5/3, 15/8 for m2, M2, m3, M3, P4, P5, m6, M6, M7; and 9/5 for the minor seventh, 25/18 for the tritone.
P2 [M]: with these ratios the Spearman correlation with the historical median order (as in t186) is at least as high as t186's 0.918, and the tritone remains the most dissonant interval.
Consequence fixed now: if P1–P2 pass, the record's music is five-limit, its ratio for each interval follows from the Tonnetz alone, and the tritone is the record's most dissonant interval because the grid has no ratio for √2.
