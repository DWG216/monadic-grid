# t148 Voices in phase: the brain at its own rhythm (pre-registered 2026-11-26, before any value is looked up)

Measure, the same as the heart's post hoc row in t147: the phase agreement of all the organ's cells at its own rhythm, R = |⟨e^{iφ}⟩|, and the voices in phase R²·N. For the heart R = 0.98 at the beat (every cardiomyocyte fires once per beat within the QRS), N = 2.5e9, so R²N = 2.4e9.
For the cortex at alpha, R factors into three published quantities: f, the fraction of neocortical neurons whose firing is significantly locked to alpha phase; r, the strength of that locking among locked neurons (spike–LFP vector strength or its equivalent); S, the agreement of alpha phase across the cortex (alpha moves as travelling waves, so distant sites differ in phase). R = f·r·S, N = 1.6e10 (Azevedo et al. 2009). Where a range is published the geometric middle is used and the range carried; sources recorded with the numbers.

P1 [M] Brain R ≤ 0.1.
P2 [M] Brain voices in phase R²N ≤ 1% of the heart's (≤ 2.4e7).
Consequence fixed now: if both pass, by the record's own measure the heart is the body's great unison and the brain sounds with a small part of itself; the brain's standing rests on chord order and poise. If they fail, the brain's alpha is a unison comparable to the heart's.
