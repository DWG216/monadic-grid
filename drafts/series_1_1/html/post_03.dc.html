Why the proton survives

What this solves

Every grand unified theory must explain why the proton does not decay. The cut of E6 into three SU(3)s, which gives the Standard Model, leaves no force that can; E6's own forces break at gravity's meeting, where they are harmless.

The danger
τ ≈ M⁴ / (α² mₚ⁵)

Forces that turn a quark into a lepton make the proton decay at a rate set by their mass M. Super-Kamiokande's lifetime limit, over 2.4 × 10³⁴ years, needs M above about 5 × 10¹⁵ GeV.

What the cut gives

The 24 forces left by the cut (three SU(3)s) carry no colour triplet: none can turn a quark into a lepton.

Where E6 breaks

At gravity's meeting, 2.4 × 10¹⁸ GeV: its proton-decaying forces sit far above any limit. Only the 27's coloured partners could show decay, if they lie below 7.9 × 10¹¹ GeV.

Foundations: why can E6 break so high?

The counting phases permute the three SU(3)s, which keeps their couplings equal at every scale. E6 is free to break where gravity meets.

Foundations: what sets the lifetime?

The coloured partners' mass. Above 7.9 × 10¹¹ GeV the proton outlives every reach; below it, its decay is the partners' signature.
