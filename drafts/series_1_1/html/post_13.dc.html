The lepton masses are one chord

What this solves

The electron, muon and tau masses look arbitrary. Their square roots form a balanced chord with a phase of 2/9: from the muon alone, the other two follow.

Koide's chord
√mk = A (1 + √2 cos(2/9 + 2πk/3))

Q = Σm / (Σ√m)² = 2/3 holds for the charged leptons, and the phase is 2/9 to 0.9σ.

What follows

From the muon's mass alone: the electron's to 1 part in 10⁵, the tau's within 0.9σ.

The quarks

Q is 0.889 for the up quarks and 0.743 for the down quarks: the quarks are chords of their own amplitude.

Foundations: why 2/3?

Balance: the chord's two parts, its mean and its swing, carry equal weight.
