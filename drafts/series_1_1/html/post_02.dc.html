A real magnet whose notes are in the golden ratio

What this solves

E8 is often called too abstract to be physics. Near its critical point, a chain of cobalt spins rings in notes whose first ratio is φ, exactly as E8 requires. The monadic grid builds E8 from the same golden steps.

A magnet that plays E8
m₂ / m₁ = 2 cos(π/5) = φ = 1.618…

In cobalt niobate the spins form chains. Tuned by a magnetic field to its critical point, a chain rings with sharp notes. Zamolodchikov showed in 1989 that their masses follow E8, the first two in the golden ratio.

Measured

Coldea and colleagues scattered neutrons off the crystal and found the two lowest notes approaching φ as the field neared the critical point.

Why it matters here

In the grid, E8 comes from the same steps run on a clock of five, where the golden ratio lives. A real material already runs E8 with φ in its spectrum.

Foundations: the clock

The grid counts on clocks: arithmetic that wraps around, like hours. On a clock of five, 2 cos(π/5) = φ appears, and E8 is built from it.

Foundations: why music?

The chain behaves like a string: its notes are frequencies, and their ratio is a chord. The grid reads masses the same way.
